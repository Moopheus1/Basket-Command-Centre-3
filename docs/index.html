WEEKLY · TOP 2Weekly Picks — ranked selection

This is a ONCE-A-WEEK decision, not a daily one. Pick a fixed day, take the two names below, hold ~20 sessions to the target or to time-exit, and do not re-pick mid-week. The backtest rebalanced weekly; acting on this panel daily is a different (untested) strategy and will churn you.

Rule: universe = CMF(20) > 0.20 AND price above MA200. Those that qualify are ranked by CMF ascending — the LOWEST qualifying CMF first. That is deliberate and counterintuitive: among names that already clear the buying-pressure gate, the most extreme ones are already extended. Ranking descending (highest CMF first) backtested materially worse (equity ×2.20 vs ×3.42). The gate is what carries the edge; maximising the gated variable works against you.

Backtest (27 weekly cycles, Jan–Aug 2026, 54 trades): 79.6% of picks reached entry + 1.5×ATR14 within 20 sessions; average +4.89% per trade; profit factor 3.55; 81.5% of weeks positive; compounded equity ×3.42 equal-weighted; max drawdown 11.7%. Split in half, the rule held up on data it was never fitted to: +4.99% per trade Jan–Apr vs +4.77% May–Aug.

Why 2 and not 3 or 5: returns decay monotonically as you add positions (out-of-sample per-trade: top1 +4.99%, top2 +4.77%, top3 +4.73%, top4 +4.12%, top5 +3.59%, all qualifiers +1.39%). Only ~6 names qualify in a median week, so "top 5" is often just taking everything and converges to the unranked result. Top 1 scored higher (×4.58) but on 27 trades, which is too few to size on.

NO STOP IS MODELLED. Target-only, time-exit at 20 sessions. Individual picks in the backtest dipped as far as −25% before recovering to target. Position sizing is entirely yours and this panel does not tell you how much to risk.

Limits, stated plainly: 54 trades over 27 weeks in a single 8-month tech bull market where simply buying every qualifier returned ×2.49. No slippage, no commissions, no position limits. This dataset has been searched many times over, so the true edge is likely smaller than shown. Treat this as a hypothesis to paper-trade forward for a quarter, not a validated system. 10% of weeks produced ZERO qualifiers — an empty panel is normal and means sit in cash, not lower the bar.

CHARTSCandles — signalled names with EMA9 / SMA20

Last 60 sessions for every name currently appearing in Weekly Picks (plus, greyed and labelled not taken, up to three qualifiers that ranked just below the cut — shown so you can compare them against the picks directly). Every Weekly name is charted regardless of where it sits against EMA9, so the comparison is always complete; Golden Cross Zone or RS Leaders that is either in the EMA9/SMA20 zone or above EMA9. Green candles close up, red close down; the orange line is EMA9, the blue line is SMA20.

Charts are drawn straight from the same OHLC bars in data.json that every panel uses — no separate feed, so what you see here matches the tables exactly. The EMA9/SMA20 position is shown because it is useful for judging entry timing, not because it was shown to work: across the 241 combined signals, "above both" hit 77.5%, "in zone" 72.7% and "below both" 75.0%, a spread well inside noise, and 88% of signals sit on the pass side. Treat these charts as a look at what you are buying, not as confirmation.

The dashed red line is the nearest resistance and the dashed green line the nearest support, found by clustering swing pivots (a bar that tops the three bars either side) within 0.75×ATR and requiring at least 2 touches. Levels beyond the reach cap — whichever is larger, 3×ATR or 12% of price — are skipped as not actionable over a ~20-session hold (the percentage floor exists because ATR/price varies from about 2% to 8% across the basket, so an ATR-only cap reaches far too little on low-volatility names); levels are taken in tiers: a cluster with 2+ touches first, then a single-touch pivot inside the band, then the window high / 60-session low, and only if nothing at all is within 3×ATR does it fall back to ±2×ATR. The tier used is printed under each chart. The second tier exists because the 2-touch rule misfires in a downtrend — a falling stock sets each lower high at a different price, so no cluster ever reaches two touches near the current level and the most relevant pivot gets discarded. Support and resistance are a judgement call, not a formula: none of this has been backtested, and a level marks where price previously turned, not where it must turn again.

Entry extension: where a name sits more than 5% above its EMA9 the tables flag it ⚠ extended (more than 8%, ⚠⚠). Across the 188 backtested signals that were above both moving averages, median drawdown before the outcome was −5.4% at 3–5% extension, −8.8% at 5–8% and −9.8% beyond 8%, with the 25th-percentile case falling from −10.0% to −17.0%. The hit rate does not drop materially (p=0.303), so an extended name is not a worse trade — it is the same trade bought at a worse moment.

#1 · 79.2% hitGolden cross zone (MA50 × MA200)

Stocks whose MA50 crossed above MA200 within the last 20 sessions (bullish regime change), or where MA50 is still below MA200 but within 2% and converging over the last 5 sessions (approaching). A cross is a lagging confirmation, not an entry signal on its own. Backtest: highest hit rate of the four signal panels — 79.2% reached entry + 1.5×ATR14 within 20 sessions (n=53), median 5 sessions, median outcome +7.29%, worst month 66.7% (the most stable of any panel). Against a 62.3% random-entry baseline that is ~+17 points of edge. It is also the ONLY panel that survives a stop-loss system test (+0.35R expectancy, profit factor 1.83, 5.6R max drawdown). Small sample (53 signals, ~7.6/month) and one regime — it lost money in October in an earlier test.

How to read “vs EMA9/SMA20”. The word is where the last close sits relative to its 9-day EMA and 20-day SMA — above both, in the zone between them, or below both. The number beside it is how far the close is from EMA9. Which of the three states a name is in carried no reliable edge in the backtest: across 241 combined signals, “above both” hit 77.5%, “in zone” 72.7% and “below both” 75.0% — a spread inside noise, with 88% of signals sitting on the pass side. The number is the part that mattered. On the 188 signals above both averages, median drawdown before the outcome was −5.4% when the close was 3–5% over EMA9, −8.8% at 5–8% and −9.8% beyond 8%, with the 25th-percentile case falling from −10.0% to −17.0% (correlation −0.308, p<0.001). Hit rate barely moved (−0.076, p=0.303). So ⚠ above 5% and ⚠⚠ above 8% are drawdown warnings about entry timing, not quality warnings — an extended name is the same trade bought at a worse moment, and waiting for a pullback toward EMA9 historically gave the same odds with roughly half the pain.

#3 · 78.3% hitBull Flag Watch (uptrend pullback, selling volume drying up, 2-3 weeks view)

Stocks in a CONFIRMED uptrend (price above MA50, MA50 above MA200 - stacked averages) that have pulled back 3-14% from their own 20-day high, with SELLING volume drying up specifically (not just total volume - average volume on down-closing days over the last 5 sessions under 85% of the prior 20 sessions' down-day average), with RSI cooled to a neutral 40-60 band - not overbought, not washed out. "Selling volume" is a standard proxy (a day's whole volume counts as selling only if it closed lower than the day before), not true tick-level buy/sell data. This is the opposite framing from Intraday Breakout Watch: instead of chasing a move already underway, this looks for stocks resting inside an intact uptrend before the next potential move, on the theory that fading selling volume during a pullback suggests sellers losing conviction rather than a real reversal. The 3-14% pullback band and the volume/RSI thresholds are reasonable starting defaults calibrated to this basket's typical volatility (most names here run 3-5% daily ATR) - they have NOT been backtested for optimal forward returns, and a pullback meeting every condition here is not guaranteed to resume higher; it can just as easily break down further. A short or empty list on a given day is expected, not a bug - all four conditions holding at once is meant to be uncommon. CHANGED: the down-day selling-volume rule no longer filters this panel — it was replaced by CMF(20) > 0.20 (sustained buying pressure). The old ratio is still shown in grey for reference but gates nothing. Backtest of the swap: hit rate 70.1% → 78.3% (n=60), out-of-sample drift +4.2 → +0.4, median max-adverse-excursion −9.19% → −6.60%, and the worst month lifted from 33.3% (below the random baseline) to 60.0%. Cost: signal count falls from 154 to 60, so expect a shorter list.

#4 · 74.6% hitIntraday Breakout Watch (volume-confirmed, 5%+ in the green, 1 week / short-term view)

Stocks currently up 5%+ on the day AND trading at least 2x their trailing 20-day MEDIAN volume (median, not mean, so one past outlier day like an earnings spike can't quietly inflate the baseline). Neither the day% threshold nor "Off Today's High" tells you whether a move is already used up — a stock sitting near its own intraday high can mean it's still climbing, OR that it already popped once and has been flat near that ceiling ever since with nothing forcing it further; the two look identical on that metric alone. "Move vs Typical Day" compares today's move to the stock's own normal daily range (ATR14) as a MAGNITUDE-only read — deliberately shown in one neutral color, not red/green, because a large number here does not mean "due to reverse" or "cut here." A stock already at 5x its typical day can absolutely run to 8x or 10x on a strong catalyst (an earnings beat, for instance) just as easily as it can pull back — this number carries no directional information either way, it only says how large today is for THIS stock compared to its own normal, which is mainly useful for comparing across tickers whose normal volatility differs a lot. None of the columns here predict what happens next. This is DESCRIPTIVE, not predictive: it only flags a move already underway. Before market close, "Volume vs 20d median" is pace-adjusted for how much of the session has elapsed, so it's an extrapolation, not a guarantee it holds through the close. The volume ratio is still shown in grey for reference but gates nothing; the 5%+ day rule is unchanged. Median max-adverse-excursion improves −13.72% → −11.14%, still the deepest of the four panels. Rotation candidates

Groups your tracked tickers by sector (from data.json) and narrows them to two setups: GOLDEN CROSS (MA50 crossed above MA200 recently, or is converging toward it) and BREAKOUT WATCH (within 10% of the 52-week high). A sector only qualifies as a Candidate if at least 30% of its tracked stocks meet one of those AND at least 50% have RSI between 40–65 — that RSI band is the "not overly bullish, not overly bearish" filter: above it you risk buying an extended move (exit liquidity for whoever got in first), below it there's no confirmed reversal yet. This is a structural filter, not a validated signal — nothing here has been backtested against forward returns, and your price history (needed for the golden-cross math) only runs about 9-10 months back, not enough to test across a full market cycle. It tells you what currently matches the setup you described, not what will happen next. Sectors with fewer than 2 tracked stocks never qualify — not enough sample to mean anything.

Death cross zone (MA50 × MA200)

Stocks whose MA50 crossed below MA200 within the last 20 sessions (bearish regime change), or where MA50 is still above MA200 but within 2% and converging over the last 5 sessions (approaching). Same lagging-indicator caveat applies.
